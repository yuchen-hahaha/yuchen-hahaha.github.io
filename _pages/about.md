I am a pulmonologist from **Taiwan** and currently a PhD candidate in **Bioinformatics and Computational Biology** at the **University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill**.

My Mandarin name is **黃于真 (Huáng Yúzhēn)**. **于** can mean “toward,” and **真** means “truth,” so I like to read my name as *toward the truth*. I guess I took the name a little too seriously.

I started in medicine, but over time I found myself increasingly drawn to the quantitative side of the questions I encountered. Now, I hope to bring statistics, mathematics, and computational methods back into medicine, using them to better understand biological and medical questions that matter to patients.

### Why I Came to Computational Biology

Before starting my PhD, I spent many years working in pulmonary medicine and caring for patients with lung disease and critical illness. My research during that time included asthma, COPD, lung cancer, sleep disorders, and other respiratory diseases.

Along the way, I kept running into questions that were difficult to answer from clinical data alone. Why do two patients with the same diagnosis sometimes have very different disease courses? Why does a treatment work well for one group of patients but not another? What biological mechanisms are hidden beneath the diagnostic labels we use in the clinic?

Those questions are a big part of what brought me to computational biology.

These days, I spend much of my time working with genomic and transcriptomic data and thinking about how statistical models can help us understand biology. What I enjoy most is taking a biological question, finding a way to describe it mathematically, and then seeing what the data can tell us.

I am also enjoying being a student again — learning mathematics, statistics, genomics, and computational methods while bringing with me the clinical questions that first made me curious about disease.

### Outside Research

Outside research, I play basketball — usually as a **point guard**. I like being the person who sees the court, moves the ball, and helps connect different parts of the team.

I also play the **drums**. I enjoy experimenting with different sounds, tempos, and rhythmic patterns, and seeing how they come together to shape the mood, energy, and character of a song.

Medicine, basketball, music, and computational biology may not seem closely related, but I think I am drawn to all of them for similar reasons: I like problems with many moving parts. I like patterns. And I like figuring out how individual pieces fit together.
